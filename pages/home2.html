GROWING TOGETHER

Small Moments at Home Can Make a Big Difference

Communication development continues beyond therapy sessions. Everyday interactions give children opportunities to listen, respond and express themselves.

Read Together

Share picture books, name objects and talk about what you see.

Make Time to Listen

Give your child time to respond without rushing or finishing their sentences.

Learn Through Play

Use games and daily routines to practice words, choices and turn-taking.

Every child is different. Your therapist can recommend activities suited to your child's individual needs.

Connection grows when children feel heard, supported and understood.

HAVE QUESTIONS?

Answers to Help You Feel Prepared

Starting therapy can bring up many questions. Here are a few helpful things to know.

Need more information?

Our team can help you explore the next steps.

Contact Us
When should I consider speech therapy?

If you have concerns about speech, language, understanding or social communication, a conversation with a qualified professional can help you understand whether an assessment may be useful.

What happens during the first appointment?

The first visit may include discussing your concerns, learning about communication history and identifying whether further assessment or support is appropriate.

How can families support progress at home?

Families can practice therapist-recommended activities through reading, play, conversations and everyday routines. Your therapist can suggest strategies based on individual goals.

Is every therapy plan the same?

No. Support is planned around the individual's communication needs, strengths, priorities and response to therapy.
