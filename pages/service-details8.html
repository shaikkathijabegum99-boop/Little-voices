UNDERSTANDING THE SERVICE

How can this support help?

People communicate in different ways. Some individuals benefit from additional tools that help them express choices, needs, thoughts, and social messages. These supports can complement speech and other communication methods.

Every person's communication profile is different. A qualified speech and language therapist can discuss concerns and help determine whether an assessment or support plan may be appropriate.

Support that starts with the individual

The aim is to increase access to communication and support the person's ability to express themselves in meaningful ways.

WHAT WE MAY WORK ON

Areas of support

Goals are selected according to the person's needs, strengths, and daily communication priorities.

  • Exploring suitable communication methods and tools
  • Building functional vocabulary for daily life
  • Learning how to access and use visual supports
  • Helping communication partners respond consistently
  • Reviewing tools as needs and skills change

    PERSONALISED SUPPORT

    More Ways to Share a Message

    Some people benefit from communication options beyond speech, including gestures, photographs, symbols, communication boards, or assistive technology. These tools can support expressing choices, asking questions, sharing information, and participating in everyday activities.

    There is no single approach that suits everyone. Support is planned collaboratively, with attention to the individual’s preferences, strengths, culture, and everyday environments.

    • Goals chosen around real-life needs
    • Encouraging, respectful activities
    • Regular opportunities to review progress
    SUPPORT BEYOND THE SESSION

    Choosing Tools That Fit the Person

    Communication supports should reflect the person’s abilities, preferences, environments, and goals. A therapist can help explore suitable options and guide families or communication partners in using them consistently and respectfully.

    Progress can look different for each person. The therapist and family can review what is working, adjust activities, and identify useful next steps together. Questions and feedback from caregivers are welcome throughout the process.
